# Late pump.fun bots find no proven edge

As of 18 October 2026, no public study, backtest or bot log shows a strategy that makes money after costs for a bot that buys 1–3 seconds after it sees a trade, pays about 5–7 % per round trip, and does not snipe the launch block. The only groups with measured, repeatable profits are structurally earlier than such a bot: deployer-funded wallets that buy in the creation block (**87 % of snipes profitable, 85 % sold within five minutes**, data from March 2025) and sandwich bots (**6.85 million attacks, 96 % of them on pump.fun's curve and PumpSwap**, Oct 2025–Oct 2026). The one rigorous out-of-sample replay published in 2026 (26,000 launches, 8–12 Oct 2026) found **every simple strategy family losing on the holdout**, trailing stops hurting in every configuration, and a "+18 % edge" that turned out to be a Mayhem-mode pricing artefact. Buying around graduation has a fat left tail (**38 % of 2,193 graduates touched −80 % within ten minutes, only ~15 % touched 2×**), and copying "top" wallets two seconds late lost under every exit rule tested. What does have measured value is **ruling coins out**: first-five-minute flow features predict death with AUPRC 0.76–0.80, deployer-funded launch buyers and known dumper clusters mark exit-liquidity machines, and wash volume inflates every volume-based signal. Several beliefs now in the bot's notes are retracted or unsupported: the Telegram 8.9× and launch-MC 4.5× graduation figures were **withdrawn by their author on 10 September 2026**; the "second launch of the day dies 64–78 %" and "quiet proven deployer graduates 12 %" numbers trace to no study (one is a vendor blog, the other has no source); and "dev already sold = safer" is contradicted by the only 2026 graduation sample. The honest verdict: the bot's own tape replay is a better evidence source than anything public, and the five things worth doing next are an artefact audit of that replay, a repeat-graduator deployer whitelist, a dumper-crew rule-out, a strict copy-trade falsification, and an exit test that should be expected to move results by only a few points. **Every number below came from search-engine extracts of pages the research sandbox could not open (only github.com was reachable); each of the six underlying notes says so at its top, and this report keeps that caveat on every table.**

> **How to read the numbers in this report.** The research sandbox's proxy blocked arxiv.org, Substack, Medium, dev.to, CoinGecko, Bitquery, pump.fun, The Block and almost every other host. Researchers could read GitHub pages in full; everything else is the text a search engine returned for a page, quoted as surfaced. Figures from blocked pages are therefore second-hand, and a few could not be tied back to their page at all (those are named as "unverified" where they appear). Treat each statistic as a lead to check from a machine with normal web access, not as a settled fact. Where a source is a vendor selling the product it describes, that is said in the text.

## Nothing public beats a 7 % round trip for a late bot

The clearest evidence comes from the only open-source project that published both its logic and an honest out-of-sample test. `apchhui/pump-research` recorded about **26,000 new pump.fun tokens over four days (8–12 Oct 2026)** with every bonding-curve trade in their first ten minutes, split the data 60/40 in time, tuned only on the first part, and reported the holdout. Early entries (0–60 s) with the best of 27,648 filter-and-exit combinations looked like +14…+16 % in selection and came out **−20 % on the holdout**. Fast flips were **−6…−7 % at any latency**, because "in the first seconds the average price is flat, and the pump.fun fee (2 × 1.25 %) is the whole loss". Entering by curve progress (20–90 % sold) lost **−4…−15 %**, with "the closer to graduation, the worse". Buying on the curve and selling on PumpSwap after graduation lost **−12…−33 %**. Buying on PumpSwap right after graduation and holding ten seconds was +7.5 % in selection and **−7.7 % on the holdout**, which the author calls "the typical signature of noise". Even a simulated same-block entry at zero latency came out −1.9 % ([pump-research REPORT.md](https://github.com/apchhui/pump-research/blob/main/docs/REPORT.md), read in full). The author's break-even move of "~7 %" at 0.1 SOL matches this bot's own cost model.

The same report contains the single most important methodological warning for this project. A filter set ("wait 30 s · dev buy ≤ 0.1 SOL · early buyers' reputation ≥ 20 · dev hasn't sold · TP +500 %, hold 9 min") showed **+18.3 % holdout ROI with a 95 % CI of +8.2…+29.5 %** and passed every robustness check. Its top trades had 90–99 % curve-reconstruction error: about 32 % of tokens ran on pump.fun's Mayhem program, whose virtual SOL reserves "jump between trades: for example 52.6 → 63.8 SOL around a 0.02 SOL buy", so rebuilding price from volumes with x·y=k is wrong for them. After rebuilding reserves as price × virtual tokens, the same strategy was **−7.5 % overall, −11.7 % on standard curves and −17.0 % on Mayhem coins**. The lesson the author draws is the one this bot must adopt: "a result that survives every statistical robustness test can still be a modelling artefact. Always inspect the individual trades behind the best numbers" ([pump-research REPORT.md](https://github.com/apchhui/pump-research/blob/main/docs/REPORT.md)).

Buying around graduation is the other place the bot hunts, and the 2026 data there is a coin flip with a fat left tail. Launchtape measured **2,193 pump.fun graduates from 10–12 Oct 2026**: **38.1 % touched −80 % within ten minutes, about 15 % touched 2× and about 5 % touched 3×**, and when one big holder dumped hard enough to crater the pool the median time was **2.2 minutes after graduation** ([Launchtape on dev.to](https://dev.to/launchtape/what-2193-pumpfun-graduations-did-in-their-first-10-minutes-and-what-it-means-for-your-bot-4f70), search extract). Its free sample shows **66 % of graduates filled their curve within five seconds of creation**, so most 2026 graduations are bundled, instant fills ([Hugging Face launchtape sample](https://huggingface.co/datasets/launchtape/solana-launch-tape)). Older data points the same way: on 41,470 graduates from Dec 2024–Mar 2025, more than **73 % fell below 40 % of their migration price within 20 minutes** ([MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1), search extract), and a June 2026 open dataset of fresh graduates and trending coins found only **about 11 % reached 2× within 24 hours even with survivorship bias**, with the best feature family adding **ΔAUC +0.0095**; its author shelved the live strategy because expected value was not positive ([ian05012 dataset README](https://github.com/ian05012/solana-memecoin-dataset), read in full). With a −12…−20 % stop that fills far below its level in a dump and a 2× tail of only 15 %, a graduation-window entry has negative skew by construction. The bot's 330-second BOOST wait sits after both the median dump and the five minutes of mechanical BOOST buying, which is the one defensible part of that trade.

Copy-trading at this bot's latency is measured to lose. A September 2026 paper-trade log followed **15 leader wallets** from tracker sites and copy services, recording the price about **2 seconds** after each leader buy with 3 % slippage and 0.5 % fee per side: every exit rule finished below 1.0× (sell at +30 s: mean 0.71×, hit rate 14.8 %; sell when the leader sells: 0.63×, hit rate 5.6 %; hold 20 minutes: 0.32×, hit rate 0 %), and "the most-copied wallets were the worst to follow", the top one being a bot with a fixed buy ladder ([pumpfun-copy-measure](https://github.com/summershameem/pumpfun-copy-measure), read in full; 54 observations over about one day). A 2025 simulation study calls copying "practically unviable" under real latency because a "slippage tax" transfers the edge to leaders and MEV searchers ([Kurnovskii](https://romankurnovskii.com/en/blog/pumpfun-copy-trading-feasibility/), search extract). The only positive academic result needed a three-agent LLM selector over coin, wallet and timing and still produced a copier return of **3 % per investment versus 14 % for the leader** under modelled frictions ([Luo et al., arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641v2), search extract). Three percent gross is negative after this bot's 5–7 % costs. A random audit of 1,161 launches (6–10 Oct 2026) found only **16.1 % of independent wallets net profitable, median −0.014 SOL, while insiders took 80.8 % of realised profit**; its pre-registered test of whether top non-insider wallets stay profitable in later weeks, "including whether copying them after a delay is net positive", reports in early November 2026 ([memescope](https://github.com/buildering-dev/memescope), read in full).

The population numbers explain why. The median Solana memecoin hold fell from about 300 s (2024) to **about 100 s (Oct 2025)** ([Galaxy via CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/galaxy-digital-report-shows-memecoin-platforms-profit-while-traders-lose-money/), search extract) and to **62 s on 22 Mar 2026** ([Dune via HTX](https://www.htx.com/en-us/news/437505/), search extract); a bot holding for minutes is trading against the flow of exits. Bitquery's count of 2.9 million "ordinary" buyers found **74 % ended with less SOL after fees**, and coins bought in the first minute were sold at a profit 34 % of the time against 28 % for coins bought a day later ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money), search extract). The headline that **73 % of pump.fun wallets were profitable in April 2026** counts realised P&L only, does not filter bots, and is attributed to losers leaving (active wallets 5.2 M → 1.8 M); Bitquery's re-run of the same month counted about 27 % ahead ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money), both search extracts, the 27 % figure not re-confirmable). The profitable-wallet share swung from 30.1 % (June 2025) to 73.3 % (April 2026), which is a regime effect, not evidence that picking got easier.

## The measured signals are about who is in the coin, not what the chart does

Where numbers exist, they concern the people on the other side of the trade. Pine Analytics traced deployers who fund sniper wallets that buy in the launch block: over about a month from 15 March 2025, **15,000+ launches, 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers, more than 15,000 SOL realised, 87 % of snipes profitable, 85 % exited within five minutes and 90 % in one or two swaps**, covering at least 1.75 % of launches (a lower bound, since only direct deployer→sniper SOL transfers were counted) ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines), search extract; coverage at [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-meme-coin-snipers-systematic-problem/)). These wallets are the counterparty to any entry inside the first minutes. The bot's v11.8 deployer-sniper rule targets exactly this group; Pine's own caveat that indirect funding paths are missed argues for also using the wallet book's dumper class rather than funding links alone.

The best early-warning classifier in the literature uses flow, not holder snapshots. "Catching the Rug" trained XGBoost on **6.4 million Solana tokens** (6.30 M pump.fun, 98 k Raydium) using 23 features from the **first five minutes of trading** (transaction, buyer and seller counts, SOL volumes, buy/sell ratios, price change, volatility, timing, plus creator attributes) and reports **in-domain AUPRC ≈ 0.76, ≈ 0.80 when the two venues are fused**, with "a vast majority" of tokens showing rug characteristics within an hour ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271v1); numbers via [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2608.20271), automated secondary summary). The label (TVL down ≥ 99 % from peak, or idle) marks almost everything, so the model is good at confirming "dead or drained" and its precision on the small "survives" class, the one a buyer needs, is unreported. For the bot this means first-five-minute flow is a reliable rule-out and an unproven rule-in. A vendor adds a wallet-based rule-out with a large claimed effect: **three or more "dump-cluster" wallets among the first 20 buyers → 94 % dump rate against a 61 % base** ([madeonsol-sdk README](https://github.com/madeonsol/madeonsol-sdk), vendor, no independent check). The bot's wallet book can compute the same count from the stream at zero RPC cost.

Buyer counts, which several of the bot's gates rely on, are the signal most inflated by organised actors. A study of **1.58 million buyer observations across 166,098 launches (12–26 June 2026)** found 1,012 persistent wallet cohorts of 2–12 wallets co-firing as early buyers. Their naive association with launch activity was +130.9 % more first-hour buyers, about half of it the cohort's own buys; after propensity matching the first-30-minute **buyer-count lift was +16.1 % (CI +13.0 to +19.4 %) and the SOL-inflow lift +6.3 % with a CI that includes zero**, and an activity-matched placebo showed a larger lift than the cohorts themselves ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795), search extract; not peer-reviewed). Rings raise head counts, not SOL. The bot's SOL-weighted `organicFlow` with repeat-size and funder-cluster exclusions is the right correction; raw "≥ N buyers" thresholds are the wrong one. Wash trading points the same way: wash-traded coins graduate **2.0 % versus 0.90 %** for clean ones ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1), search extract), **21.4 % of 30.8 M pre-migration transactions were wash trades** ([MELT, arXiv 2602.13480v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v2), search extract), and **82.89 % of tokens with >100 % returns showed artificial growth** across four chains ([Mongardini & Mei, arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2), search extract). Big movers are usually manufactured, so a volume or trending gate without an organic filter actively selects manipulated coins.

Creator history is the one selection dimension where the claimed magnitude is big enough to matter even after heavy discounting, and also the one where the bot's current numbers have no measured backing. What is documented is descriptive: the most active deployer created **3,357 tokens of which 16 graduated (≈ 0.48 %)** ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/19808), press, source not shown), one wallet cluster earned **more than $5 M launching hundreds of coins without marketing** ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/token-creator-made-over-5m-launching-hundreds-of-memecoins)), and in the October 2026 random audit **66.9 % of creators sold their entire position** ([memescope](https://github.com/buildering-dev/memescope)). On the positive side a vendor reports that **34 "elite" deployers graduate about 71 % of launches against a 1–2 % base**, "30× to 70× the baseline" ([MadeOnSol deployer intelligence](https://madeonsol.com/blog/inside-deployer-intelligence-pump-fun-scoring), vendor, no methodology, its own deployer counts conflict across pages). No peer-reviewed study measures next-launch outcomes conditional on creator history. The direction is consistent across every source; the effect size is unmeasured, which is why it belongs at the top of the test list rather than in the default config.

The common thread is label trouble. "Graduated" is the only widely studied outcome, and it is not the outcome a buyer needs: graduation odds are raised by wash trading and self-buys, and a graduate's median post-graduation path is a dump. The June 2026 dataset whose author shelved the project because "live expected value was not sufficiently positive" is the clearest statement that snapshot features at detection time barely predict 1 h/6 h/24 h returns ([ian05012](https://github.com/ian05012/solana-memecoin-dataset)). Kamat's 15-day → 14-day calibration failure is the clearest statement that whatever works today drifts within two weeks ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823)). Both argue for the bot's existing habit of judging each strategy on its newest trades, and against hand-coded points for any launch covariate.

## Widely used filters with no measured effect, and exits that barely move the result

Every trading terminal exposes the same fourteen or so columns (age, MC, liquidity, volume, buy/sell counts, holders, top-10 %, dev %, snipers %, insiders %, bundled %, pro-trader count, Dex paid, socials) and none publishes an outcome-tested threshold. The numbers traders repeat (snipers ≤ 10 %, dev ≤ 10 %, top-10 ≤ 20–30 %, holders ≥ 10–100, liquidity ≥ $5 k, MC ≥ $11 k) are personal or affiliate presets ([Meme Coin Navigator](https://memecoinnavigator.com/best-axiom-trade-filters-for-new-pairs/); [CoinCodeCap](https://coincodecap.com/best-settings-for-bullx-bot), search extracts). The only vendor-published cut-offs are GMGN's `rug_ratio`, `bundler_rate` and `wash_trading` above 0.3 = high risk ([gmgn-skills SKILL.md](https://github.com/GMGNAI/gmgn-skills/blob/main/skills/gmgn-token/SKILL.md)) and Bubblemaps' bundled-supply bands (< 5 % low concern, 5–15 % moderate, one cluster > 20–30 % red flag) ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull-using-bubblemaps-before-it-happens/), search extract). No precision or recall exists for RugCheck, Bubblemaps, SolSniffer, GoPlus or any terminal's rug score. GMGN's default "mint and freeze authority renounced" check filters nothing on pump.fun because the factory already guarantees it, which is why behavioural checks carry all the load there ([DeFade](https://defade.org/memecoin-rug-check), search extract).

Three specific filters deserve a named verdict. A raw **snipers %** cap is mostly noise after controls: the cohort study above shows sniper rings pile into launches that were already attracting buyers, so what matters is whether early buyers are still holding and whether they are deployer-funded, not how many bought in the first seconds ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)). **Dex paid** has zero outcome data either way, traders disagree on it, and it is now auto-purchased from the first $300 of a coin's fees on at least one launchpad, so it is a weak effort signal at best and never a gate ([dexpad](https://dexpad.lol/); [CopyTradeHQ](https://copytradehq.com/axiom-filters) vs [Meme Coin Navigator](https://memecoinnavigator.com/best-axiom-trade-filters-for-new-pairs/)). **Narrative or meta fit** has no backtest anywhere; the one commentary with a number says a narrative is born, peaks and goes "legacy" in under 72 hours ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/memecoin-market-structure-2026-narratives), search extract), and the Telegram effect that stood in for "socials matter" is withdrawn. Keep narrative soft, as the owner's `minNarrative` 0.35 already is. **KOL count** is a trailing indicator and a hidden-size problem: KOLs often buy through Jito bundles with side wallets, so a visible buy understates exposure and a visible sell may be a slice ([CabalSpy README](https://github.com/CabalSpy/CabalSpy-MCP-Server), read in full); a Change.org petition and a DL News piece allege leaderboard farming and pre-announcement insider buys around Kolscan ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/memecoin-kols-bag-millions-as-pump-fun-buys-kolscan-tracker/)). Nothing found argues for loosening the bot's "KOLs only trigger a check" stance. Hour-of-day and weekday effects have no public study at all; the only data-backed statement is that deployer-funded sniping clusters in US business hours ([Pine Analytics via BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-meme-coin-snipers-systematic-problem/)), which says nothing about which side wins when.

On exits the public evidence is thin and humbling. The 26k-launch replay found "the spread between the best and the worst exit is only ~3 percentage points", with distant take-profits (+200…+500 %), no trail and long holds doing best and "none of these overcomes costs" ([pump-research](https://github.com/apchhui/pump-research/blob/main/docs/REPORT.md)). No published memecoin backtest of trailing stops or TP ladders exists; Jupiter's July 2026 trailing-stop launch comes with one hypothetical and a plain statement of the trade-off ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jupiter-adds-trailing-stop-loss-to-limit-orders), search extract). Generic volatility logic says a stop tighter than the typical bar range is shaken out by noise ([Cryptohopper](https://www.cryptohopper.com/blog/13240-how-to-set-a-stop-loss-that-survives-crypto-volatility); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/atr-based-stop-distance.md), search extracts), and with 15–30 % intra-minute moves a fixed 10–15 % stop is inside noise unless it is volatility-scaled, which the bot's `exit.stopLoss` already does. Practitioner guides converge on "sell 25–50 % at 1.5–2× to recover principal, scale out 25 % per further leg, keep a 5–20 % moonbag" and publish no results ([MadeOnSol exit guide](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-memecoin-exit-strategy-guide); [DEXTools tutorial](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/memecoin-trading-strategy-take-profits), search extracts); with the bot's own history showing only 11 % of trades reaching 1.4×, a ladder whose first rung is 2× leaves most trades to the stop. The honest reading is that exits cannot rescue picks: the bot's own Money map (internal, Oct 2026: 7,365 coins, 11 % ever reached 1.15×, median −5 %, even perfect exits only +3.2 %) says the same thing as the public replay.

Costs set the bar every idea must clear. The curve charges **1.25 % per side**; a PumpSwap round trip on a small-cap coin costs about **2.4–2.5 % in protocol and creator fees** alone, falling to ~0.6 % only above ~98 k SOL MC (about $8 M) ([pump.fun docs/fees](https://pump.fun/docs/fees) via search; [Blockworks on Project Ascend](https://blockworks.co/news/pumpdotfun-fee-model)). Constant-product arithmetic (the researchers' own, not sourced) puts price impact at about 0.6 % average slippage per side for 0.5 SOL in a fresh ~80 SOL pool and 2.5 % in a 20 SOL pool, doubling for 1 SOL, which justifies the bot's 3 %-of-liquidity size cap and removes the cost advantage of bigger trades below ~80 SOL of depth. Sandwiching is concentrated on these venues (**6,850,144 attacks by 2,545 bot wallets on 2,154,994 victims, 96 % on pump.fun, $29.7 M gross, Oct 2025–Oct 2026**), small per attack (~$4.3 gross by the researchers' arithmetic) but a tax on any wide slippage tolerance ([Bitquery via CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/), search extract); the owner's 20 % terminal tolerance is exactly what blind sandwiching exploits. About **33 % of non-vote transactions failed in a 36-block sample on 14 Sept 2026**, mostly bot spam ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/solana-transaction-failed-check/), search extract). The most rigorous landing study found priority fees and Jito tips barely change landing time and stake-weighted QoS matters most ([Chorus One, Dec 2024](https://chorus.one/reports-research/transaction-latency-on-solana-do-swqos-priority-fees-and-jito-tips-make-your-transactions-land-faster), search extract), so a 1–3 s bot gains more from fresh blockhashes and a direct sender than from bigger tips. All of this adds to a realistic **5–8 % round trip at 0.5–1 SOL, a break-even of about 1.06–1.10×**, consistent with the bot's own "~7 % at 0.2 SOL, needs ~1.15× to break even".

Regime drift deserves its own line because it decides how long any test result stays valid. The graduation base rate moved from 0.26 % (mid-June 2026) to 6.7 % on one day (29 July 2026) when BOOST changed launch incentives ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/pump-fun-graduation-collapse-solana-fees-2026); [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/defi/2026-07-29-pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives-409815), search extracts); pump.fun activity fell ~80 % in the three months to June 2026 and then set a 2026 record of $828 M in one day in October ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/pump-fun-activity-plunges-80-193800825.html); [pump.fun PUMP page](https://pump.fun/pump-token), search extracts); Holder Rewards replaced Cashback on 12 Sept 2026 and stock-priced quote pairs went from 53 to 4,270 launches a day between August and September ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/pumpfun-holder-rewards-cashback-deprecated/); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-stock-tokens-meme-coins), search extracts). Platform mechanics move the odds far more than SOL's price does, so the bot's regime signals should key on its own trailing 24–72 h graduation rate, launches per hour and profitable-wallet share, not on SOL trend or a Fear & Greed index, which no study has tested against pump.fun outcomes.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question the owner should ask on 7 November. It is not "has the bot found the right rules yet" but "is there any residual edge for a late, fee-paying, non-insider participant once the exit-liquidity machines, wash factories and sniper rings are filtered out". No public source has shown one; the only measured winners are faster or better-informed, and the only honest positive lead (repeat-graduator deployers) is a vendor claim waiting for the bot's own data to confirm or kill it. That makes the TradeTape replay, the Money map and the copy replay the bot's primary instruments, and it makes the artefact audit the first job, because the one rigorous public replay shows how a fully "robust" result can be an accounting error.

The second shift is in what counts as a signal. Chart shape, buyer counts, socials and list membership are all either manufactured, inflated or withdrawn; the signals that survived scrutiny describe people (deployer-funded buyers, dumper crews, creators' records, proven wallets) and the first five minutes of flow. The bot already stores most of these. The work is to turn them into tested rule-outs and one or two tested rule-ins, retire the magnitudes that were never measured, and keep every result dated, since the market re-prices its own rules every few weeks. If, after that, the paper edge is still not positive on a few hundred trades, the evidence says to believe the tape rather than to tune again.
